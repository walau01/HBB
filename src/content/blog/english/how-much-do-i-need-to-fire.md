## Start with spending, not savings

FIRE stands for financial independence, retire early. The first question is not how much you have saved. It is how much a year of your life costs.

Write down what you spend in a normal year, then add the things that are easy to forget: insurance, car replacement, travel, and support for family.

## Turn spending into a target

A common shortcut multiplies yearly spending by a fixed number, often 25. That multiplier comes from a withdrawal rate. A 4% yearly withdrawal implies a portfolio 25 times your spending.

The shortcut is a starting point. It does not know your age, your tax situation, or how long your money has to last.

## What changes the number in Malaysia

A finished article would look at EPF savings and when they can be withdrawn, healthcare costs before and after 60, and spending in ringgit while some investments are priced in other currencies.

## What would change my mind

- [ ] A longer retirement than the one the shortcut assumes
- [ ] Spending that rises faster than general inflation
- [ ] A large one-off cost in the first years

## The rule in one sentence

Withdraw 4% of your portfolio in the first year of retirement, then raise that amount with inflation each year.

## Where it comes from

The idea comes from studies of historical United States stock and bond returns in the 1990s. They asked which starting withdrawal rate would have lasted through about 30 years in most past periods.

## What it assumes

The original research looked at a particular mix of stocks and bonds, a particular market, and a fixed retirement length. Fees, taxes and currency were simplified or left out.

## Why it matters for early retirement

Someone retiring at 40 may need their money to last 50 years or more. A finished article would compare what the evidence says about longer periods, lower starting rates, and flexible spending rules.

## What would change my mind

- [ ] Evidence from markets outside the United States
- [ ] Results that include realistic fees for a Malaysian investor
- [ ] A spending rule that adjusts after bad years
